Use the measured monitor refresh instead of 97% of it. Frames are not scheduled faster than the screen.

On: never generate more frames than the screen can show. Off: generate as many frames as the GPU budget allows; the screen still shows at most its refresh rate (the newest frame wins) and the counter reports generated frames.

On: Auto generates fewer frames on fast scenes to hide interpolation artifacts, so the output rate swings with the content. Off: Auto keeps the highest multiplier the GPU budget allows.

Re-checks the motion on fences, railings, window grids and other repeating patterns, where the network can lock onto the wrong period and the pattern doubles during pans. With Anime mode on it is more aggressive (right for drawn content, slightly worse on live footage). Costs a little GPU time per source frame; off reproduces the network's motion as is.

With Anime mode. Anime is drawn on twos and threes: a door, a walking crowd or a slowly turning head gets a new drawing only every 2nd or 3rd frame and stands still in between. This spreads every drawing step evenly over the frames it is held for, so the motion slides instead of stepping. Every frame on screen is then a generated in-between, the real drawings included: on small steps this looks smooth, on big steps (a character walking close to the camera) the in-betweens carry the generator's softness and edges can double. Off keeps the real drawings as they are, as 1.x did. Uses one frame of lookahead (one source frame of delay). Needs periodic-structure repair.

Samples the source frames bicubically instead of bilinearly when building each generated frame, so moving edges, hair and fine texture come out crisper and closer to the original. Clear win on drawn and live video; a touch softer-is-safer on game capture, where you can turn it off. Costs about 0.2 ms per generated frame.

The size the network runs at. Balanced is the model's native scale; higher settings cost more GPU time and rarely look better.
